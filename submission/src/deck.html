Since August 2026, a restaurant-funded offer starts only after the owner's OTP

July–August 2026

Bengaluru restaurant associations object to discounts deducted without clear consent, and ask for approval for a fixed period and itemised payouts.

8 August 2026

Zomato agrees: restaurant-funded campaigns are opt-in and start only after the owner approves them by OTP.

Now

An owner who cannot see whether an offer pays has every reason to stop approving it.

TrueLift adds four calls to the offer flow Zomato already has

1

Quote

Before the OTP

A forecast of extra orders and profit with a range, and a better version of the same discount, or no offer.

2

Serve

A customer opens the menu

A table lookup decides who sees the discount; a random 1 in 10 eligible customers is held out.

3

Payout

Every week

The restaurant pays for its own offer, itemised. Zomato co-funds only what it measures.

4

Measure

Every week

Held-out against shown customers: the extra profit with a range and a verdict, on WhatsApp.

No new screens: the forecast sits on the OTP screen, the result arrives on WhatsApp, and nothing changes in how customers order.

Inside Zomato

Who sees the discount

When a customer opens the menu, a table lookup decides whether to show the discount. Regulars who would order anyway do not get it.

A random 1 in 10 eligible customers is held out and sees normal prices, never higher ones. That control group is what makes the weekly measurement possible.

The visitors are synthetic people.

Approval and every week

The owner approves what was shown, then sees what it really added

The OTP stores the forecast, its range, who pays and the four-week period in a hash-chained ledger, so every deduction can be checked.

Every week: the extra profit with a range and a verdict, keep, change or stop, beside what today's report would show. Renewing needs a new OTP.

What is still not right, and we say so

Everything is simulated

Customers, orders and profits are simulated on real 2019 listings. Real numbers need Zomato's data; the first step inside Zomato is a shadow holdout.

Suggestions look better than they are

A suggested version's stated advantage is about {{selection_inflation}} times its true one, though {{selection_truly_better}} truly beat the owner's. The page says so next to every suggestion.

Small restaurants lean on the model

The four-week estimate over-states the truth by {{draws_bias}} an offer on average, inherited from the model while a restaurant's own control group is small.

An audit of our own work found 50 issues; each is fixed and guarded by a test. Every number in our documents is checked against the reports by a ledger of {{claims_count}} claims.

From prototype to pilot: measure first, change who sees offers later

1

Shadow holdout

2% of eligible customers held out of offers owners already run, in one city. Nothing changes for owners.

2

Measure

The forecast and weekly result for a random half of the pilot restaurants: {{pilot_per_arm}} in each half, {{pilot_total}} in all.

3

Target

Owners in that half may let TrueLift choose who sees the offer.

4

Scale

City by city, with the same guardrails: sample-ratio checks, placebo offers, renewal rate.

Each phase starts only when the one before has passed its checks. The service answers a menu decision in {{lat_serve_p95}} ms (95th percentile) over HTTP on a laptop.
