# Comprehension cards

Print each card on its own half-page, large type. Show one at a time and ask the question under it
without explaining anything first. Mark the answer on the note: read correctly unaided, correctly
after one hint, or not understood. Write down what the owner says in their own words.

The numbers are an illustration for one ₹400 order, not a result. They follow from
`truelift/economics.py`: at full price the restaurant keeps ₹134 of a ₹400 order; with ₹60 off it
keeps ₹91.70, so each order that would have come anyway costs ₹42.30, and the offer pays only if
32 of every 100 orders using it are extra.

---

## Card 1: the same week, two reports

**Today's offer report**

> ₹60 off on orders above ₹299 · this week
> Orders that used the offer: 142
> Discount given: ₹8,520
> Sales from these orders: ₹48,280

**TrueLift's weekly result**

> Orders that used the offer: 142
> Extra orders it created: about 41. The other 101 would have come anyway.
> Extra profit for you: about −₹510 this week.
> This offer is probably losing you money.

**Ask:** "Was this offer good for your restaurant this week?"

**Read correctly** if the owner says, in any words, that the offer lost money although many orders
used it (most of those customers would have ordered anyway). **Hint, if needed:** "Look at the line
about orders that would have come anyway."

*For the interviewer:* 41 extra orders × ₹91.70 = ₹3,759.70; 101 orders that would have come
anyway × ₹42.30 = ₹4,272.30; the difference is −₹512.60. 41 of 142 orders is below the 32 in 100
the offer needs.

---

## Card 2A: a forecast as a range (odd-numbered interviews)

> Before you approve: ₹60 off for everyone
> Extra profit for you: about +₹900 a week
> Likely between −₹300 and +₹2,100

## Card 2B: the same forecast as a chance (even-numbered interviews)

> Before you approve: ₹60 off for everyone
> Extra profit for you: about +₹900 a week
> About 8 in 10 chance it makes you money

**Ask:** "Will this offer make you money?"

**Read correctly** if the owner says it probably will but might not (a small loss is possible).
**Not understood** if the owner reads it as a promise of ₹900, or as a likely loss.

*For the interviewer:* both cards say the same thing. With a best estimate of ₹900 and an 80%
range of −₹300 to +₹2,100, the chance of a profit is about 83%, so "about 8 in 10". The demo shows
both together; this card tells us which one owners read better on its own.

---

## Card 3: one in ten customers held out

> To measure your offer, 1 in 10 customers who could use it will not see it.
> Comparing them with the customers who do see it shows how many orders the offer really added.
> You do not pay a discount on their orders.

**Ask:** "What happens to the customers who do not see the offer, and why would we do this?"

**Read correctly** if the owner says those customers still order at the normal price, and that it
is done to see what the offer really adds. Then ask: "Would you accept this for your restaurant?"
and note the answer and the reason.

---

## After the cards: the demo on the phone

Open the offline demo, choose a restaurant like theirs, and hand over the phone on the create
screen. Ask them to make the offer they would normally run and to read the forecast card aloud.
Note where they hesitate. Do not use the guided demo here; it explains too much.
